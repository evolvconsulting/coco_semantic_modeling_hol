DEALER 360 FICTIONAL CALL TRANSCRIPT

Participants:
  DEMETRIAN TITUS — Data Owner
  MATAURUS ADEWALE — Part of the team building the view

MATAURUS: Hey... thanks for hopping on. I know the team is interested in building a semantic view over the dealer
data so people can just ask questions in plain English instead of pinging you
every time. Can you walk me through what we've got?

DEMETRIAN: Sure. It's really one story: application,
funding, servicing, and the dealer master table is the ground truth. Every other table points back to a dealer in dealer master eventually.

MATAURUS: Okay so dealer master is the master record of every dealer, and the other tables help fill in the picture?

DEMETRIAN: Right, one row per dealer. Then the application events table is one row per
application, that's the top of the funnel. Some of those get declined, some
get approved but the customer finances elsewhere, and some actually get
booked with us. Only the booked ones show up in another table we've got, the funding events table.

MATAURUS: So funding events isn't every application or even every approved application, just applications we end up funding.

DEMETRIAN: Yeah, one row per contract that actually made it. And once a
contract's funded, we make a record in teh servicing events table, that's the monthly
"is this loan being paid on time" table.

MATAURUS: Ok... so does servicing events relate back to dealer master straigt from the dealers id... or 
does it join back to funding events, which then links it back to dealer master?

DEMETRIAN: Go through funding. Servicing doesn't know anything about the
dealer relationship, it only knows about the loan. The dealer
attribution really belongs to the contract itself. If we joined straight to the dealer you lose the fact that it's
riding on top of a specific funded contract, so if we ever needed to ask the agent to trace a
bad loan back to which application caused it, it couldn't do that if we joined staight to the dealer table.

MATAURUS: Alright, what's next? 

DEMETRIAN: We've got out dealer performance table... that one's a monthly rollup, already pre-aggregated per dealer. 
It's the dashboard-ready version so nobody has to compute look-to-book fromraw applications every time.

MATAURUS: Let's talk about that. When I'm building this out I have to decide what's
something you'd group by versus something you'd just total up. Volume, for
instance, thats the number of applications a dealer had that month right? 

DEMETRIAN: Yeah that's it. Useful for grouping dealers by tier. We can group dealers per volume so
we're not comparing a dealer who sees 700+ apps per momth to a dealer who may only see 300. Different standards between the two for sure. 

MATAURUS: Makes sense.

DEMETRIAN: And things like look-to-book, funding velocity, exception rate, those are
different. Sometimes we want the average across some slice, like this dealer over
three months, this tier this quarter, whatever. It's also useful as a snapshot at a given time, but more useful to average across a certain time frame.

MATAURUS: Okay, so those are the ones that actually get aggregated. What's the
short list of numbers that show up in literally every review you run?

DEMETRIAN: The ones I just mentioned. Look-to-book, we just call it conversion rate
half the time, that's the share of applications that turn into a funded
deal. Funding velocity, which is just days to fund, is how long from signing to
actually funding. Exception rate... some people say stipulation rate, same
thing, is the share of deals that get held up on paperwork. And application
volume, obviously.

MATAURUS: And for look-to-book, funding velocity, exception rate, we're typially averaging all those over a specified time? 

DEMETRIAN: Averaged for sure. Summing a rate doesn't mean anything.

MATAURUS: What about charge-off contribution? I see it sitting right next to those
in the performance table.

DEMETRIAN: It's fine to expose the raw number.

MATAURUS: Ok, gotcha. 

DEMETRIAN: Yeah, so the goal is here point in time intellegence. Lets say one of our tier 1
dealers, y'know, big volume, which should be tracking within the tier 1 baselines for our north star metrics, started
sliding. Let's say Look-to-book went from holding within the baseline to shifting down significantly. 
Lets also say funding velocity more than doubled, past the point where dealers start getting annoyed and routing 
deals elsewhere... we want to know this stuff in real time. It's a lot tougher to deal with this after the fact instead of being able to catch it early.

MATAURUS: Yeah makes perfect sense. 

DEMETRIAN: That's the idea, anyways. 

MATAURUS: Thanks!
